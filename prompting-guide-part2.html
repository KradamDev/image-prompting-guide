Dialing In the Machine

Beyond the prompt itself lies a set of numerical controls that govern how the AI interprets, refines, and reproduces its output. Understanding these settings separates deliberate creators from those who simply press Generate and hope.

Section 01 — Steps
01 / STEPS

Steps — How Many Times the AI Thinks

When you press Generate, the AI does not produce a finished image in a single calculation. It begins from a field of structured noise and progressively sculpts that noise over multiple passes — each pass called a step. At each step, the model compares the current state of the image against your prompt and nudges the result closer to what you have described.

A lower step count produces output quickly but leaves the image under-refined — textures appear soft, fine details are absent, and the composition may feel loosely formed. A higher step count grants the model more opportunities to commit to detail, yielding sharper and more coherent imagery. That said, returns diminish substantially beyond a certain point: the visual difference between 50 and 100 steps is far less significant than the difference between 10 and 28.

The practical approach most experienced users follow is to generate at a modest step count — around 20 to 28 — until a satisfying composition emerges. Only then do they commit to a higher step count for the final render. This conserves time and resources without sacrificing quality in the end result.

💡
Many platforms offer free generation for images produced at 28 steps or fewer within a standard resolution range. Staying within this threshold during the exploration phase is a sound habit, particularly when iterating through many prompt variations in a single session.
Section 02 — Prompt Guidance
02 / PROMPT GUIDANCE

Prompt Guidance — Obedience vs. Creativity

If Steps controls how long the AI refines an image, Prompt Guidance — also labelled CFG Scale or Guidance Scale on some platforms — controls how strictly the AI adheres to your instructions during that refinement. It functions as a dial between creative liberty and disciplined compliance.

At low guidance values, the model treats your prompt as loose inspiration, interpreting its spirit while feeling free to introduce its own creative variations. Results are often dreamlike and painterly, though less predictable. At high guidance values, the model holds your prompt as a rigid specification and tries to satisfy every element you have described — producing crisper, more literal images, but risking over-saturation and visual harshness at the extremes.

Guidance Value Reference — V1 & V2 Models
Range Behaviour Best Used For
2 – 6 Loose, interpretive. High creative freedom; dreamlike results. Abstract art, landscapes, mood pieces
7 – 14 Balanced adherence. Reliable character and scene rendering. General-purpose; the majority of prompts
15 – 20 Strict adherence. Sharper results but prone to colour artifacts. Precise compositions; pair with Decrisper
21+ Overly rigid. Unpredictable artifacts; generally counterproductive. Experimental use only
V3 Models — Different Defaults
Newer Models Prefer Lower Guidance

If you are working with a V3-generation model, the recommended guidance range is considerably lower — around 5 to 6. These models are trained to be far more responsive to prompts at lower values, meaning a setting that felt moderate on a V1 model may feel aggressive on a V3 one. Always consult the documentation for your specific model, as optimal defaults evolve across generations.

Supplemental Guidance Controls

Prompt Guidance Rescale

When guidance is set high on V3 models, colours can become over-saturated — a harsh, "deep-fried" effect with extreme contrast. The Rescale slider corrects this by rebalancing colour distribution without lowering guidance itself. Adjust it incrementally whenever high guidance values cause garish or unnatural colouring in your output.

Decrisper

Decrisper is a toggle designed to mitigate visual artifacts and harsh edges that manifest at higher guidance values. Unlike Rescale, it affects the image at any guidance level and can be used as a deliberate stylistic choice — some users prefer the softer, more organic quality it imparts even at moderate settings.

"When an image is largely correct but one small detail is off, try locking the seed and nudging the guidance value by a single point in either direction. This targeted adjustment often resolves minor issues without disrupting the overall composition."
Section 03 — The Seed System
03 / THE SEED SYSTEM

Seeds — Reproducibility & Controlled Iteration

Every image an AI generates begins from a unique random starting point — a specific pattern of noise that the model sculpts in response to your prompt. The number that initialises this random pattern is called the seed. Two prompts that are otherwise identical will produce the same image if they share the same seed, and entirely different images if their seeds differ.

This mechanism is one of the most practical tools in a prompter's workflow. By locking a seed after finding a composition you are satisfied with, you can make isolated, deliberate changes to your prompt — adding a tag, adjusting guidance, or modifying a style descriptor — and observe precisely how each change affects the image, without a randomly shifting starting point confounding your results.

Section 04 — Strength & Noise
04 / STRENGTH & NOISE

Strength & Noise — Transforming Existing Images

The settings discussed so far — Steps, Guidance, and Seed — govern how the AI generates an image from a blank starting point. Two additional controls become essential when you move into image-to-image workflows: that is, when you upload an existing image and ask the AI to modify or expand upon it.

Strength

Determines how much the AI is permitted to depart from the uploaded image in response to your prompt. At maximum strength, the AI may reconstruct the composition substantially. At minimum strength, it treats the uploaded image as nearly untouchable, making only the subtlest prompt-driven adjustments.

Noise

Controls the degree of randomness the AI may introduce into areas it is filling in — particularly sparse backgrounds or regions with minimal detail. Higher noise enables the model to invent richer, more varied content; lower noise preserves what exists and introduces very little that is new.

These two controls interact in ways that are not always intuitive at first. A practical starting point is to begin with both settings low and raise them one at a time, observing the effect of each in isolation. This methodical approach prevents the common mistake of attributing an undesired change to the wrong setting.

⚠️
Running multiple consecutive generations with high noise can compound artifacts — each pass introduces new randomness that incrementally degrades image coherence. If you intend to refine an image across several generations, keep noise moderate and lower it gradually rather than repeating at maximum values.
Section 05 — Enhance & Resolution
05 / ENHANCE & RESOLUTION

The Enhance Feature & Resolution Strategy

Once you have a generation you are satisfied with at a modest step count, most platforms offer an Enhance feature — a second-pass processing step that applies your prompt again to the existing image, refining it further without restarting from noise. Think of it as giving the AI an opportunity to revisit and polish its own work with fresh attention.

The Enhance feature typically combines Strength and Noise into a single Magnitude slider for simplicity, though most platforms also expose the two settings individually for finer control. A low-magnitude pass will tighten details and smooth artifacts while remaining close to the original composition. A higher-magnitude pass introduces more significant refinements — and proportionally more risk of unwanted divergence.

Resolution & Aspect Ratio

The resolution and aspect ratio of your canvas are not merely technical parameters — they actively shape the composition the AI produces. A tall vertical canvas naturally encourages full-body character framing and emphasises height. A wide horizontal canvas favours landscape scenes and group shots where subjects appear side by side. Choosing your canvas dimensions before generating is a deliberate creative decision, not an afterthought.

Horizontal / Landscape

Wider compositions suit group scenes, environments, and panoramic settings. The AI naturally distributes characters side by side and gives environmental elements more horizontal room to develop.

Common ratios: 16:9  ·  3:2  ·  4:3

Vertical / Portrait

Taller canvases encourage full-body character renders. Note that using the tall tag alone is often insufficient — pairing it with a vertical resolution produces far more consistent results.

Common ratios: 9:16  ·  2:3  ·  4:5
Part II — Recommended Workflow
A Repeatable Process for Every Session

  • Set your canvas resolution before generating. Choose an aspect ratio that matches the compositional intent of your scene — landscape for environments, portrait for characters.

  • Generate at 20–28 steps with a guidance value of 7–12 to explore compositions efficiently and without unnecessary resource expenditure.

  • Lock the seed of any generation showing genuine promise. Note it down before moving on — seeds are easy to lose track of during rapid sessions.

  • Iterate with the seed locked, adjusting a single element of your prompt or one setting at a time to isolate the effect of each change.

  • Raise steps and apply Enhance only once the composition, characters, and scene elements are satisfying at the structural level.

  • Use low-magnitude Enhance passes to refine detail, reserving high-magnitude passes for intentional and substantial stylistic transformation.

Part II — Summary
Five Controls, One Coherent System

Steps determines how thoroughly the AI refines your image — use 20–28 for exploration and higher values for final renders. Prompt Guidance sets the AI's adherence to your text — a value of 7–12 is a reliable starting point for most models. Seeds give you reproducibility — lock any composition worth building upon. Strength and Noise govern image-to-image transformation — treat them as independent variables and adjust methodically. Resolution shapes composition before the AI even begins — choose your aspect ratio deliberately.

Together, these five controls form a coherent system. The prompt defines what the AI creates; the settings define how it creates it. Mastering both is what transforms AI image generation from a lottery into a genuine craft.
